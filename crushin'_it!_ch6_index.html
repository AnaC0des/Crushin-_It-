Shane - January 2018

He had planned this date for weeks, but wanted everything to be perfect. He slipped his phone into his pocket, grabbed his wallet and keys, slid his shoes on, and headed out the door.

Shane arrived at the theatre thirty minutes ahead of when Troy was supposed to meet him. He met with the owner and confirmed the movie was set for 9 p.m. as planned. With everything in place, Shane returned to the lobby to wait for Troy’s arrival.

Five minutes later, Troy walked into the theatre’s lobby, wearing a light gray dress shirt tucked into dark wash jeans. He locked eyes with Shane, and the smile on his face turned Troy’s knees to jelly. If they weren’t in public… Troy looked around and was surprised not to find lines or a crowd at nine on a Saturday night.

“Hey, baby. You look gorgeous!” Shane said, taking Troy’s hand in his and kissing it fondly. His eyes locked on him, soft and loving.

Shane’s affectionate greeting in the public space was strange, but stranger still was the fact that they were all alone.

“Hey…Shane. Where is everybody?” Troy asked, a nervous grin on his face.

“Remember how you said you wanted to sit in a theatre with me and not have to worry about being seen or having to hide?”

“Yeah, I told you that at dinner a few weeks ago.”

“Well, Troy, would you like to see Crazy Rich Asians with me?”

Troy held Shane’s hand a little firmer, deliberately, pulling him in for a kiss. “I’d be honoured, babe,” he replied against Shane’s lips, his eyes molten with love.

Shane led him towards the theatre, but Troy paused at the concession stand.

“So how do concessions work?” he asked, noting the lack of staff around.

“You can have whatever you like. Everything’s paid for.”

Troy grabs two boxes of Junior Mints and a large Coke, foregoing his usual movie staple extra buttered popcorn, knowing Shane hates the hulls getting stuck in his teeth, and follows Shane to the theatre.

They sit in the fifth row, dead centre. The leather seats are spacious, recline easily, and have lots of leg room.

Just as the previews begin, something occurs to Troy. “How did you manage to book the whole theatre for the night? I mean, I know you’re Shane fucking Hollander and the city loves you — rightfully so — but…yeah, how?”

“So the owner was at one of our home games last year, and she couldn’t get her car to start. Mel in Security asked if anyone had a set of jumper cables, and I never leave home without them. I gave her a jump, and she tried to give me money, but I couldn’t take it. So, she said she’d just owe me a favour.”

Troy knew Shane was a good person — kind, generous, thoughtful, sweet, loving, selfless — but every single time Troy saw it in action, he felt less deserving of Shane’s affection. That voice in his head was really loud as Troy stroked Shane’s cheek, in awe of the man next to him.

“Thank you. You are easily the most generous man I have ever known, Shane. I am completely awed you’re even real.”

“I can prove it,” Shane says, leaning into Troy’s palm and kissing the calloused muscle there. “If you want me to, I can prove to you I’m as real as you are.” Shane leaned in, and Troy closed the gap, kissing Shane languidly until the movie started.

About one hour and a box of Junior Mints later, Troy is straddling Shane’s lap as they make out, the movie acting as background noise to the love story playing out in front of the screen.

Groundhog Day 2018

“Okay. I’ll be home in fifteen minutes. Does that work for you?”

“Sure. I’m half an hour away. I’ll see you when I get in.”

They said their goodbyes, and Shane tried to calm his racing heart. He could do this. He could have this sensitive conversation with Ilya, then spend the rest of his weekend with his boyfriend, banking time together for when they’d be on the road next month.

When Shane pulled into Ilya’s driveway, the garage door was open. Ilya unloaded groceries from his Mercedes-Benz GLE, turning around as Shane pulled up.

“Sorry, I didn’t anticipate long lines at the checkout,” Ilya explained as Shane approached.

“Thanks, that’d be great.” Shane felt himself loosen up already as Ilya pulled a can of Canada Dry out of the fridge and popped it open before handing it to him.

“Have you swapped ginger ale for Cokes since I last saw you?” Shane asks, noting the absence of Cokes in Ilya’s fridge.

“No, I just ran out of Cokes.” Ilya pulled a twelve-pack out of the grocery bag and slid them onto the shelf next to the ginger ale. “Please sit down. Are you hungry? I can make you something.”

“No thanks, I had lunch before I came.” Shane took a seat on a barstool at the island in the centre of the kitchen.

Shane sipped his ginger ale, the cold fizzy beverage familiar on his tongue. Why am I here again? Shane asked himself as he watched Ilya’s back muscles flex in his black Bad Bunny concert tee, putting bags of Cheetos away in a cabinet, leaving one on the counter.

“Troy said you didn’t do it for him. That’s because you did it for me.” Shane’s eyes welled with tears he struggled to hold back.

“Just because I’m not your dance partner doesn’t mean you shouldn’t dance at all.” The sincerity in Ilya’s eyes as he confirmed the nagging thought in Shane’s brain — the voice that whispered almost two years ago — was now screaming in his mind.

He stuck his hands under the warm flowing water, rinsing the majority of the cheese dust off his fingers before pumping soap into his palm and washing more thoroughly. Ilya passed him dry paper towels, took the used ones and threw them in the trash.

Troy

Shane didn’t tell Troy he was coming to the game, so it was a surprise when the commentators announced his presence over the speakers during the first intermission. Troy blushed as he looked up in the VIP section and saw his boyfriend waving. It was Troy’s best game yet as a Centaur with a goal and two assists to his credit.

After the game, Troy found Shane waiting for him outside the locker room. He had been in the habit of arriving first and leaving last since he was in Juniors; thankfully, that habit afforded him and Shane the privacy to leave the arena hand in hand.

Troy noticed Shane was quieter than usual, pensive, tense despite the 4-2 Ottawa win over the Raiders. “Something wrong, babe?” Troy asked, not that this was the time or place to discuss it properly.

“No. Well, nothing to discuss now, at least.”

He knew Shane was a terrible liar, a trait that only comes from leading an honest life — which Troy appreciated. But it was especially telling tonight. Usually Troy called him on it, but he wasn’t in the mood to get into the details of what irked Shane in the Canadian Tire Centre parking lot.

Shane

The next few weeks were a blur. Shane increased his therapy appointments, processing the guilt he felt over his lingering feelings for Ilya and his breakup with Troy, so he could focus on hockey. It was a journey he’d continue past All-Star Weekend, as his mind was prone to ruminate over in his low moments.

All-Star Weekend came, and for the third consecutive year, Shane was voted as Captain of his team. Team Canada versus Team USA was the chosen theme, putting Shane and Ilya on the same team. Shane explained to Ilya that he wasn’t ready to resume their relationship, which Ilya understood and supported.

But something as deceptively simple as a relationship label didn’t stop the love and chemistry from showing when they were together. The league quickly realized they were more entertaining as teammates than they were as competitors. Since the game was in San Jose, Rose came to cheer him on and hang out before he returned to Montreal.

The All-Star Game promotional material loved the Hollander-versus-Hunter dynamic. The old guard versus the next generation. It wasn’t about the hockey — the game was merely an opportunity to sell tickets during a slump in the season — but the camaraderie between players. On the ice, there was prime chirping material as the league’s biggest bad boy and golden boy double-teamed the legend.

“Hey, Scott, is it nap time already?” Shane said after he scored his second goal of the game.
