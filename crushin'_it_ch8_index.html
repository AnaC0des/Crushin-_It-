Shane - April 2018

Immediately after his accident, Shane recuperated at his parents’ house in Ottawa. While he was grateful for their support, he missed the independence he had at his house. The constant — but well-intended — reminders to rest and mind his screentime wore on him after a week.

Yes, he still wore a sling to take the strain off his collarbone while it healed, and he still got headaches as a result of the concussion, but he was getting better. He stepped down from the heavy-duty painkillers that made him loopy to a high-dose over-the-counter pain reliever that allowed him to be more alert.

Ilya visited frequently, breaking up the monotony. He read Shane’s hockey books to him, cooked Russian comfort foods, and updated him on league news. Without Shane, Montreal didn’t make the playoffs that year. Ottawa, although improved from last year, didn’t make it either, affording Ilya more time to be with Shane.

Jackie Pike organized the Metros WAGs to bring food to the Hollanders on a daily rotation. Hayden always said Jackie runs a tighter ship than the Navy, and Shane saw it firsthand. He also had a steady supply of fresh lilies by his bed every week. There was never a note with the bouquet, but Shane always knew who sent them.

After the second week at his parents’ house, Shane couldn’t take it anymore. Then, Ilya had an ingenious suggestion: Shane could stay with him. Ilya had experience caretaking for his father when he was sick, would keep Shane occupied, and he’d be close to the Hollanders. David and Yuna weren’t entirely sure Shane and Ilya would do more than fuck all day, but they managed to behave under their roof.

So, Ilya helped Shane pack — or more accurately, packed what Shane told him to — and drove him home. Walking through the garage door into the house, Shane was reminded of the last time he was here. The nerves, uncertainty, and lust from that day came flooding back. Ilya noticed and turned around to face Shane.

“What’s the matter, malysh?”

“I just remember the last time I was here. It was pretty confusing, actually.”

“Then, we just have to fill this house with good memories to outweigh it, yes?” Shane smiled as Ilya kissed his cheek. He always knew just how to bring Shane back to himself.

“Yes, we will.”

“Let me show you to your room.”

“My room? You mean we’re not…sharing?”

Shane shook his head, then regretted it when it started to throb. He followed Ilya up the steps and down the hall until he opened the door to the primary suite.

“If you’d rather not share with me, then you’re welcome to stay in the guest room. But I’d rather keep you close, so I know you’re not lying on your right.”

“God, I love you so much, Ilya.” Shane cupped Ilya’s face in his hand, pulling him in for a kiss.

Shane had been at Ilya’s for two weeks, and they got into a domestic rhythm together. Ilya cooked, and Shane was his favorite taste tester. They played cards, and Ilya’s over-the-top reactions made Shane laugh.

Hayden came over, and — to Shane’s surprise — he and Ilya were civil, perhaps out of consideration for Shane’s recovery. But in the quiet moments when his mind turned over the situation, they acted like a couple without actually being one, which baffled him.

“Why are you doing this?” Shane asked Ilya while he read from a massive volume on the history of hockey.

“Hm? What do you mean? I’m reading this boring book you like. I can’t figure out why, it’s just dates and events.”

“I mean, this isn’t exciting, fun, or sexy. This is rote, gritty, and boring. Wouldn’t you rather be out clubbing with Svetlana or hooking up with whoever captures your attention for a night?” Shane asks, with wet eyes on Ilya’s disappointed face.

“Shane, I love you. You’re injured, so I’m here helping you get better. No, it’s not glamorous or exciting, but we’re together. That’s what matters to me. You called me your boyfriend in the hospital.

“This is what boyfriends do. So, next time you wonder if it’s a burden for me to remind you about your meds, drive you to the doctor, cook dinner, or literally anything else you need, just know there’s nowhere else I’d rather be.”

“My boyfriend?” Shane asked softly, smiling as Ilya took his hand, drawing tiny circles with his thumb.

“Yes, solnyshko, your boyfriend. I think that fits what we are to each other, right?”

“Yes, I think it fits us perfectly.”

The kiss they shared then was unlike any they’d had before. It was a breath shared between two halves of one soul as it sighed in relief.

Ilya

Ilya’s been living a dream. Shane has spent the last two months living with him after his injury. He wishes it didn’t take a gut-wrenching injury to get here, but Ilya isn’t questioning his luck either. One evening after dinner, he put on a playlist of his favorite ballads, stood up, and offered Shane his hand.

“May I have this dance?”

“Ilya, I’m not sure I should be dancing. I’m not fully healed yet…”

“Nothing fancy. Just let me hold you.”

“Well, okay. I trust you.”

Ilya took Shane in his arms, holding him so closely he could feel Shane’s heartbeat. “Lights Down Low” started, and they swayed to the beat as Ilya slowly turned them in a circle. As the song went on, Ilya felt Shane’s body melting into him, their hearts beating in time with each other. He sang the words under his breath, the lyrics seemingly made for their story.

“This sounds a lot like us,” Shane observes, echoing Ilya’s thoughts.

“I was just thinking the same thing.”

“After ten years, I’d be disappointed if I didn’t know what you were thinking.”

“But it makes life exciting, yes? Spices things up?”

“If it were anyone else, it would be terrifying. But with you, I know I’m in good hands.”

The song fades out, and “From This Moment On” begins.

“Oh my god, I love this song!” Shane snuggles a little closer, his head resting on Ilya’s shoulder.

“Dykstra recommended her, said she’s the best to come out of Canada. His tastes are a little niche, but he was right about Shania.”

“I grew up listening to her music: she was on the radio all the time, and my mom had all of her albums. She even performed this song with the Backstreet Boys.”

“How can I forget my boyfriend has a crush on the Backstreet Boys?” Ilya says with a chuckle.

“Shut up.” Shane says, laughing too.

“They are very crush-worthy! Hot men singing about love, I get it. Any song of theirs I hear, I think of you.”

“Who needs the Backstreet Boys when I’ve got a real hearthrob dancing with me in his living room?” Ilya presses his lips to Shane’s forehead, his heart growing a hundred times larger at the words.

That weekend, Shane flips through the movies in Ilya’s favorites on Netflix, searching for one to watch.

“Oh, I haven’t seen The Notebook in years! Let’s watch that!”

“Ilya, are you sure that won’t be…triggering?”

“I always cry when I watch it, but it’s a beautiful story about enduring love, Shane!”

“Let’s see what else is here.”

“Oh, stop! I love Sam Claflin!”

“Me Before You? Ilya, I love Sam too, but as Finnick. This is going too far.”

“You’re no fun!”

“Ilya, you picked the saddest movies!”

“Like you have room to talk, Mr. Titanic? I’m Russian! All our art is about suffering and dying. I can handle it, Shane!”

“I’m the one who’ll be putting you back together after one of these movies tears you apart. And I don’t have the strength for that right now, so what else can we watch?”

Ten minutes later, Shane lies back against Ilya on the couch as they watch High School Musical, debating which character the other is.

“Are you sure Troy Barrett isn’t more of the Troy Bolton here?”

“No, I am Troy. You’re Gabriella.” Ilya insists.

“Gabriella? No way! I’m Chad. You’re Sharpay!”

“Now you’re just speaking nonsense. But she is fabulous.”

“That’s the second movie, Ilya.”

Somewhere in the middle of that mundane argument, Ilya thinks this could be their life all the time. Where Shane doesn’t have to go back to Montreal at all, just stay here in the home Ilya made for them.

Shane - June 2018

Shane and Ilya reclined on the couch together watching the Eastern Conference finals to see if New York or Carolina would be competing for the Stanley Cup this year. Shane predicted Carolina, arguing they’ve been consistent all season. Ilya countered that New York would be unbeatable since the league’s best two players were out, and Grandpa Scott Hunter had a hot new boyfriend.

Earlier that day, Shane’s physical therapist said he was on track to return for next season. This was the goal he worked toward three times a week, but he didn’t want to leave Ilya in Ottawa. After all of the challenges they fought to get here, it finally felt like they were on the same page. Could they really make it work in two different cities?

On screen, Scott Hunter scores another goal, furthering the lead New York has held since the second period.

“Looks like I was right. Grandpa’s going back to the Championships,” Ilya mused.

“You don’t know that. Carolina can still pull it out. They’re only down by two.”

“Not with that defense. They may as well sit down and watch, because they’re not stopping anything.”

Fifteen minutes later, the final score was 6-3, sending the Admirals back to compete for the Cup.

“It was Carolina’s first time in the Conference finals in ten years. Once they get used to being there, they’ll play better.”

“Yes, and Hunter wants to kiss his boyfriend on the ice again.”

“Don’t we all?” Shane said, turning to kiss Ilya.

“I don’t know. I have a prettier boyfriend I’d rather be kissing.”

“Oh my god, I didn’t mean I wanted to kiss Kip, you asshole!” Shane said, dissolving into a fit of giggles as Ilya covered his face in kisses.

“Then, I’ll just have to win the Cup next season so I can call you down from the stands to center ice and kiss you.”

“No way, the Metros are winning it next season! But don’t worry, baby. I’ll still give you your on-ice kiss.”

A week later, Shane walked into the kitchen following his morning run to find Ilya sitting at the kitchen island, staring at pencil sketches of a woman. Each sketch looks slightly different; some are made with more pressure on the pencil, others with more refined details. The version in Ilya’s hand now resembles a rough draft of the others before him, but the woman’s curls, the mole on her cheek, and her sharp features mirror the man holding it.

“Is this…?” Shane asks softly, afraid of intruding.

“Yes. My mother.” As Ilya spreads the pictures out more, Shane counts eight different drawings, each tear-stained in different places. “I didn’t have pictures of her in Russia, and after she died, my father forbade me from drawing her. Every year, it gets harder to remember her face, even when we have the same face.”

Shane stepped behind Ilya, wrapped his arms around his waist, pulling him close against his chest. His thin cotton tank clung to his chest, translucent with sweat; his skin was sticky, and the smell of unwashed gym socks emanated off him in waves, but Ilya was more important than that. Ilya leaned back into Shane’s embrace, letting his boyfriend support him in this.

“How did she die?” Shane asked softly into Ilya’s curls.

“By accident. She accidentally swallowed a whole bottle of pills.” Shane felt something wet drip onto his arms and realized Ilya was crying.

“How old were you?”

“Twelve. I found her.”

Shane’s heart broke then, pouring out of his eyes and down his cheeks. For fifteen years, Ilya carried this pain inside him while his father and brother judged him, bullied him, despite doing his best to support them. Not anymore. Now, Shane was here to help Ilya carry the grief.

“Ilya, I’m so sorry.” The sentiment carried so much Shane couldn’t articulate right now, but would find a way to say later. Sorry I didn’t know. Sorry I made that stupid comment back in 2011 on that rooftop in Vegas. Sorry you carried this alone for so long. “What was she like?”

He felt Ilya inhale against him, tension melting from his muscles.

“She was so funny and beautiful. She had the prettiest laugh, like music. And she gave the best hugs, like she could pour all her love into them. Maybe she did.”

Shane held Ilya closer. It dawned on Shane that Ilya reflected the best of his mother in every way he just named.

Ilya’s story about his mother haunted Shane all day, lingering in the moments he and Ilya cooked together, discussed the Stanley Cup finals, and eventually went to bed. This wonderful woman gave him his soulmate — and she had died tragically. Shane couldn’t let Irina’s story end here. He had to bring her story out of the darkness and into the light.

He couldn’t go back in time to help her, but how many people found themselves in a similar place, in need of tools and support to navigate their mental health? It kept Shane up until he shook Ilya awake at three in the morning and told him his plan. He wanted to start a charity funded by kids’ hockey camps to support access to mental health resources for kids and adults of all ages.

In the following weeks, Shane and Ilya pitched the idea to Yuna, working out the logistics of starting a charity. They had friends they could ask to help coach during the off-season, and soon what started as an idea blossomed into a legally recognized charitable organization. Named in honor of Irina Rozanova, Ilya’s late mother, the Irina Foundation was created.

Ilya - September 2018

Ilya stood with Shane in the restroom outside of the conference room they rented for the occasion. His black made-to-measure suit looked immaculate, but he couldn’t stop tugging at his sleeves. A lot of his life was about to change. He was finally telling his mother’s story publicly after being told for years not to mention it. Ilya would also come out to the world — both as bisexual and as Shane’s boyfriend — after hiding from Russia’s homophobic influence.

“Hey, it’s going to be okay.” Ilya felt Shane’s hands on his shoulders, their gentle pressure eased the tension there until he was back to his usual ease. He looked so handsome in his navy suit. Their reflection caught the corner of Ilya’s eye in the mirror; together, they looked so right. “I’ll be right beside you the entire time. I’d never let you do this alone, Ilya.”

“Thank you, moy lyubov. I wouldn’t be here without you.”

“I doubt that.” Ilya leaned in to kiss Shane’s blushing cheek, watching it redden further.

“Please, this was all your idea, take the credit for once! You’re amazing.”

Despite the unlocked door, there was a knock before David emerged.

“They’re all set up. How are you both feeling?” He looked from Shane to Ilya, who looked at each other with nervous smiles.

“About as ready as we’ll ever be, I think.”

“I’m nervous, but this time I think it’s good.”

David walks over and places a kind hand on each of their shoulders.

“I want you both to know I’m so proud of you. For the foundation, for your professionalism, and even more for the way you support each other. You’re both so dedicated to making the world a better place that it’s been a privilege to watch you both grow into the men you are today.”

Tears came easily to Ilya as David gave him the affirmation his father never did. He wraps his arms around the older man, hugging him as if this is the only way he’ll hold his emotions together.

“Thank you, David. This means so much more than I can say, in English or Russian.”

Ilya releases him from the embrace, and Shane steps into the space Ilya just vacated, hugging his father.

“Thanks, Dad. I think we needed to hear that just now.” Shane takes a step back, checking his suit one last time before exchanging a look with Ilya. “We’re ready.”

Ten minutes later, Shane and Ilya are standing before the press at a wooden podium. As promised, Shane stands just to Ilya’s left, close enough that he can feel his presence without crowding him.

Ilya looked out into the audience and spotted some familiar faces: Yuna and David Hollander, Coach Wiebe, Hayden Pike, and, surprisingly, Scott Hunter flew in to attend. Ilya locked eyes with Scott and smiled. For all his chirping, he respected him as a person and was grateful for his support.

“Good afternoon, thank you for coming. My name is Ilya Rozanov. You probably know me for my work in the MLH as a center and Captain for the past eight years, but I’d like to tell you a little more about who I am off the ice.

“My mother, Irina Rozanova, was a kind and loving woman. She could always make me laugh when I was sad, but she was plagued with her own demons. She had depression and nowhere to turn for help. When I was just twelve years old, my mother died by suicide. My father forbade my brother and me from talking about it. He told us ‘She had an accident,’ and left it at that, but there was so much I didn’t understand, and I was only left to wonder.

“As I got older, I discovered I am bisexual. I’ve been fortunate to live and work in welcoming countries. I’m from Russia, where my community is treated with contempt, forced to hide, and punished for being outspoken.

“In addition to repressing queer voices, there are inadequate resources for supporting mental health in Russia. Even here in Canada, it can be difficult for someone to access the help they need. It is with this goal — expanding access to mental health tools and support — that my boyfriend, Shane Hollander, and I created the Irina Foundation. More people should have access to the help they need in their darkest times. Shane will tell you more about our initiatives. Thank you.”

There were murmurs around the room as they switched places at the podium. It was a relief to say those words into the microphone: to tell his truth, his mother’s truth, and rewrite the narrative his father spun so many years ago.

Ilya listened proudly as Shane described the foundation’s mission and values, and explained the camp’s logistics. A ten-million-dollar endowment would fund the camps, and the profits would be donated to organizations that provide access to free mental health resources.

The first camps would kick off next summer during the MLH off-season for two weeks in Ottawa at the Centaurs' rink. They had eight coaches already signed on: Max Riley, Leah Campbell, Ryan Price, Hayden Pike, J.J. Dagenais, Wyatt Hayes, Shane and Ilya.

Afterward, they faced anticipated questions (“How long have you been dating?”, “Has your romantic relationship ever affected your hockey?” “Do you plan to expand the camps beyond Ottawa?”) and some they didn’t (“Is this a sign you’re planning to retire soon?”, “Will we ever see you both on the same Conference team?”)

Ilya made sure to include Martine Thibodeaux in the press pool after they impressed him in the wake of Shane’s hit on the ice last spring. It was important to have queer media outlets represented, and Open Play would be one of the first to break the story.

When the guests had all left, the press dispersed to report what they’d seen and heard; Ilya took Shane’s hand in his and turned to face him.

“Let’s go home, moy lubimyy.”
