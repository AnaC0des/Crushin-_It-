Shane - March 2018

Montreal is playing Ottawa in an afternoon game to a sold-out crowd. The Metros are up 2-1 in the second period, and Shane has the puck. He prepares to pass to Hayden, but he’s not in position. Shane sees Ilya’s face fall in slow motion as the world suddenly spins around him.

Wait, that can’t be right. The world is always spinning. So why is it so blurry? Why does my chest hurt? Why does Ilya keep circling?

“Ilya! Ilya!” Shane calls, his voice garbled and weak.

Why doesn’t he respond when I call to him? Why is Hayden dropping his gloves?

Shane feels himself being lifted onto a gurney. The EMTs are talking to him, shining a light in his eyes — why are there so many lights? Ugh, it’s too bright! Wait, Ilya!

“Tell him I’m fine. He’s gonna worry.” Shane mumbled, beginning to drift out of consciousness.

“Shane, we need you to stay awake now. Your coach is calling your parents. Don’t worry,” the paramedic reassures him.

“No, not them. Ilya. Tell him I’m fine.” Shane’s blinks get slower, and his body feels heavier, and the paramedics are telling him to keep his eyes open.

But he’s just…

So…

Tired.

Ilya

Seeing Shane lying out on the ice rattled Ilya. The referee directed him back to the Centaurs’ bench, but he couldn’t think about the game. He couldn’t think about Hayden Pike throwing himself at Dillon, swinging recklessly because he was distraught, too. He couldn’t think about Troy fighting the refs to get to Shane’s side, risking being thrown out of the game entirely for his behaviour. He couldn’t think about anything or anyone but Shane.

And yet he had no reason he could give to leave. He and Shane weren’t partners; they were starting to be friends, but that’s not good enough to convince an MLH coach to let his Captain leave halfway through the game. Not that Ilya made a difference. He played on autopilot, but the Metros were unstoppable, winning the game 5-1.

Unfortunately, Ilya was delayed in getting to the hospital. Jenn asked him to field a few questions from the press — an inhuman request at a time like this. Ilya thought about channeling his second-favourite athlete of all time and giving a flat “I’m contractually obligated to be here, but I will not answer a single question” to every reporter, but then it came to him. If he had to be here instead of at his beloved’s bedside, Ilya was going to make Shane proud.

“Mr. Rozanov, that was a nasty check in the second. How are you holding up?” asked the reporter from ESPN.

“It was a shock to all of us. I know Dillon is terribly sorry for it. He didn’t mean to hit so hard, but it was a bad angle, too fast. It is a dangerous sport. Could’ve happened to anyone, really.”

“Ilya, Karlie Draper, NBC Sports. You looked pretty upset on the ice after Hollander got hit. What was going through your mind?”

Ilya took a deep breath and tried to steady his voice as he replied. “Shane Hollander has been set up as my biggest competitor since we competed for the International Prospect Cup in 2008. He is the best hockey player in the league, and he is my friend. I just want him to get better.”

The final question was far from what Ilya was expecting. A journalist from a niche queer sports blog that worked their way up to the Metros’ press room was an impressive accomplishment. Ilya vaguely remembered sending them a video message last year for an interview with Shane.

“Martine Thibodeaux for OpenPlay. You spoke to your mutual respect for Mr. Hollander. You also said your teammate Tanner Dillon didn’t mean to hit him so hard. Can you confirm whether this was a personal attack on Shane Hollander based on his race as an Asian-Canadian or his sexuality as an openly gay player in the MLH?”

“I have known Tanner Dillon for over a year now. He is not a hateful man. He is kind and accepting of everyone for who they are, on and off the ice. This was an accident, Tanner said he is sorry, and we all hope Shane is back soon. Thank you, no more questions.”

Ilya left the press conference as quickly as possible. He was about to call an Uber when his phone lit up with “Jane” as the caller. Ilya didn’t think twice before accepting the call. “Shane?” Ilya asked, voice quivering with worry.

“Holy shit,” came a familiar voice on the other end. Montreal’s fifteenth-best player.

“Pike? What the fuck?!”

“Rozanov? Oh boy. We’ll unpack that later, but yes, it’s Hayden, calling from Shane’s phone. Listen, I knew ‘Boston Lily’ would want to know this, but Shane’s getting some scans taken. If you come to Montreal General in about an hour, I might be able to get you some time alone with him on the fourth floor.”

Ilya almost couldn’t believe his ears. It was so much to process. Did Hayden Pike really say he could come see Shane?

“Pike, are you serious? Do not play with me. I am not in the mood.”

“Yes, I mean it, Rozanov. I’ll send you the address. One hour.”

“Thank you, Pike — Hayden. This was very nice of you.” Ilya’s throat tightened, and the words almost didn’t make it out.

“You’re welcome. See you soon, ‘Lily.’”

Ilya quickly requested a ride, and he arrived at the hospital twenty minutes later. He took the stairs at Hayden’s advice, meeting him on the fourth-floor landing.

“Jesus, I still can’t believe it’s you,” Hayden said as Ilya pulled him in for a tight hug.

“Thank you. Thank you for calling me. I have no idea how I would’ve found him otherwise.”

“No problem. First, all the guys are in the waiting room, and Shane’s parents are in the room with him now. I don’t think this is how you want to meet the Hollanders. Mama Yuna is a force of nature in the best of circumstances. This is closer to the worst.”

“Fine. Then what are we going to do?”

Troy

Fuck.

He just had to fight the refs to get to Shane. He hadn’t been able to reach his ex-boyfriend, who lay on the ice—only got himself removed from the game entirely. Well, it’s not like they needed him. Montreal would be impossible to beat now, buoyed by the need to avenge their Captain.

How in the hell was Rozanov holding it together? he wondered, watching Ilya on the ice from the penalty box. As far as Troy knew, he and Shane had reunited last month, the catalyst for their breakup. It still hurt to think about, but Troy wasn’t mad at Shane, Ilya, or even himself; he was mad at the circumstances in which they all found themselves.

In an alternate universe, where the space-time continuum split like in one of Wyatt’s comics, he and Shane would still be together. He’d meet the Hollanders, and Shane would meet his mother and Charlie. They’d move in together — Troy would move to Montreal if they would have him — so Shane wouldn’t have to see his ex every day. Maybe one day, a few years in the future, he’d buy a ring, kneel, and ask Shane to be his husband. All the things Troy dreamed of when they were dating, they could have in another world, another timeline.

But right now, Shane was en route to a hospital, and Troy didn’t know where or how he would get there. He was absorbed in the infinite possibilities he would never have when a voice suddenly took him out of his thoughts.

“I’m sorry, dude. I know he was your boyfriend at one point. I didn’t mean to hit him that hard. I swear, I didn’t mean to hurt him!”

Tanner was sobbing, his whole body shaking with the force of emotion. Troy figured he must look equally as messy: sweat gluing his hair to his forehead, tears streaking down his face, snot smeared across his upper lip from where he wiped it on his sleeve.

He couldn’t hold it against Tanner. It was the nature of the game, the kind of hit players get up from all the time. But the angle, the speed, a slight miscalculation — that was the difference between walking it off and waking up in a hospital hours later. If Shane taught Troy anything, it’s that you can’t hold grudges. You gotta let that shit go, or it’ll drag you down the mire with it.

“I know, Dillon. I forgive you. It was just bad luck, that’s all. I know you didn’t mean it.” Tanner seemed to cry harder hearing Troy’s words, and he grabbed his hand, holding on for dear life.

“Thank you. You’re a good man, Barrett.” Troy patted Tanner’s back, recalling how Shane made similar comments about Troy months ago, his heart breaking all over again.

The game ended, and Troy boarded the team bus, searching the web for updates about Shane — where he was hospitalized, his current status, his prognosis, anything. Not finding any news, Troy turned in his seat, did a quick headcount, and noticed the Captain was missing. When they got back to the hotel, Troy tapped out a quick text.

Ilya

Ilya stepped into Shane’s room at Montreal General Hospital, scared of what he’d find there. The last thing he expected was for Shane to be alert and speaking.

“Ilya!” He trilled in a higher register than usual. His brown eyes sparkled with joy as if this were a social call to his house.

Hayden was running interference with the Hollanders, taking them on a coffee run so Ilya could visit Shane privately. It wouldn’t buy a lot of time, but Ilya would take what he could get.

“Hi, Shane. How are you feeling?” Ilya asked as he cautiously approached his hospital bed.

“Better now you’re here. Oh, Ilya, I’ve missed you so much! You have no idea!”

“Shh, someone will hear you.”

“Ooh, Ilya, are you being naughty? It’s okay, I won’t tell anyone.” Shane’s giggle made Ilya’s stomach flip, and he forced himself to hold it together just a little longer. “You’re so far! Come closer. Please?”

Shane reached out to him, and Ilya stepped closer, unable to resist Shane begging for anything. He clasped Shane’s left hand in both of his, wishing he could stay with Shane until he was released. His right arm was in a sling, causing Ilya to worry more.

“Better,” Shane sighed, looking angelic in the fluorescent lights.

“Did… did the doctors say what’s hurt?” Ilya faltered over the words, English failing him as he felt so much at once.

“A concussion and fractured collarbone. I’m out for the playoffs but…could’ve been worse.”

“Dillon is very sorry, by the way. He didn’t mean to hurt you.”

“It happens. We all get our bell rung eventually. I’m more upset that he ruined my plans. I was gonna ask you to come over tonight.”

“It’s okay. I’ll see you when you’re out of the hospital.”

“I had a whole date planned. Dinner, candles, the Lady Gaga and Tony Bennett album you like. And I was gonna ask you to be my boyfriend.”

Ilya’s breath caught in his chest, freezing him in place. He knew these were the ramblings of a man on heavy painkillers, but he was stunned. Only a month had passed since Shane broke up with Troy, a decision which devastated both of them. He’d been clear about needing space to process the end of the relationship before jumping back into bed with Ilya, a boundary he was careful not to cross.

“Shane, let’s discuss this when you’re feeling better, hm? Maybe in a week?” Ilya offered, trying to find the most polite way to say: Let’s not define the relationship when you’re high as fuck.

“Okay, you can think about it. But don’t take too long. I miss you! I wanna kiss you!”

“Alright, just one. I have to get going before your parents get back.” Ilya leaned in to peck Shane’s lips until Shane opened his mouth, his tongue probing Ilya’s lips until he returned the favour.

When they heard the door open, it was too late. David and Yuna walked in to see Shane French kissing his famed archrival. The couple broke the kiss quickly, Shane letting out a nervous giggle.

“Oh, no,” he lamented, rolling his eyes back to Ilya. “Our fun’s over.” Ilya smiled and squeezed his hand.

“Hi, I’m Ilya,” Ilya started, unsure of where to take this.

“And what are you doing here, Ilya, kissing our son?” The woman — presumably the Mama Yuna that Hayden had referred to — seemed greatly displeased to see him.

“Mama, we’re in love!” Shane sing-songed. “I couldn’t say it for so long, but now I can, and it feels GREAT!!”

Oh, boy. There it was. Well, nothing for Ilya to do but put their minds at rest.

“Mr. and Mrs. Hollander, it’s true. I do love your son. I love him so much that it hurt me seeing him on that ice. I had to be here to see he was alive, breathing, with my own eyes. I had to feel he was still here with my own hands. And thanks to Hayden, I am here, and I know the man I love will be okay.”

Yuna and David exchanged a look, words passing between them silently. After a proper introduction, Yuna still seemed dubious of the relationship, quietly assessing the man holding her son’s hand.

“So, Ilya, how did you and Shane get to be so close?” Yuna’s hawklike focus holds Ilya’s gaze until he smiles, looking over at Shane fondly.

“Shane introduced himself at the International Prospect Cup in 2008, and since then, the rivalry has been more of the media’s work than ours. We like competing; it pushes us to be better, but we never hated each other.

“And in 2010, CCM called me for a photoshoot — promos ahead of the MLH season — and I suggested they include Shane. They loved the idea, got Shane onboard— ”

“Got me onboard, actually,” Yuna insists. “As his manager, I signed off on that endorsement deal.”

“Then, thank you for agreeing to do it. Shane and I had our first date in Toronto because of it. We’ve been together on and off ever since.”

“Let me get this straight: you’ve been seeing Shane — my Shane — on and off for eight years? So, are you saying you’re back on again? And for how long? How can I be sure you’re not hurting my son?”

“Yuna,” David starts, placing a gentle hand on his wife’s knee. “Maybe we should wait to ask about their plans when Shane’s better able to contribute to the discussion. We can’t leave it all up to Ilya to explain; it’s not fair. Right?”

David looks over to where Shane has drifted off to sleep, then over to Ilya, still clutching Shane’s hand on the bed. His gray eyes are softer than Ilya’s own father’s had ever been. He understands Shane better now: how he gets his grit, his steely resolve from his mother, but he gets his optimism and softness from his father.

“Right. Thank you, David. I should go, let you have some time alone with Shane. I’ll be back in the morning. I wish we had met under better circumstances.”

Surprisingly, Yuna opens her arms to him, and Ilya sinks into her embrace. He had gone fourteen years without a mother's hugs, and getting one now after watching his beloved Shane’s injury was a balm to Ilya’s soul.

Already emotionally raw, Ilya was prepared to just shake David’s hand, but the older man extended his arms to him as well. Ilya nearly fell into David, he was so overcome with emotion.
