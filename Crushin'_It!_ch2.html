He informed his agent, Farah Jalali, and together they coordinated his next steps. Shane met with Montreal brass to inform them of his upcoming public statement and his desire to do so in his uniform. They had been vocally supportive, but it wouldn’t behoove them to be anything else since their star player was also a trailblazer for queer BIPOC hockey players who may come after him.

He wasn’t a social media fan by any measure, but he made an Instagram account with one explicit purpose: to introduce himself to the world properly. Shane spent the last week searching through family photos with his parents for the right ones to tell his story. He’d sent a draft of the post to Farah on Thursday, who replied with a teary-eyed thumbs-up. One Month Later

Ilya had liked Shane’s coming-out post as soon as it crossed his feed. He’d sent a DM telling Shane how proud he was that he was living his truth. Ilya knew it wasn’t an easy journey for Shane but he was brave for stepping into the light anyway. Just as he’d stepped up to introduce himself nearly a decade ago.

Ilya also saw the posts from fans thirsting over Shane. It was easy to see why with his soft brown eyes, dancing freckles, charming smile, and the love he had for his team and their fans. Shane never turned down a kid who asked for an autograph or a picture. He played every charity game, was humble and genuine in interviews, and never spoke ill of anyone. Shane Hollander was as good as gold.

And damn did Ilya miss him like crazy. He and Svetlana were packing Ilya’s bags for All-Star Weekend in Tampa, FL, but this time his heart wasn’t in it. Usually, Ilya loved playing in Florida. So many Boston fans vacationed in the Sunshine State, and others transplanted there for tax advantages and scrumptious views. But not even the thought of fans screaming his name could lift his spirits.

Sveta noticed Ilya’s uncharacteristic slump in his shoulders, the way he responded a beat slower than usual, the way he just seemed…lost. She dropped the tropical printed shirt she was folding into the half-filled suitcase and looked Ilya square in the eye.

“Okay, Ilyusha. What’s going on?”

Ilya stopped once he caught the heavy way Svetlana held his gaze, one eyebrow raised to stop any bullshit from attempting to pass her discerning eye. How was Ilya gonna explain this? He sighed and started with a sliver of the truth.

“I’m in love, Sveta. But…he doesn’t love me back.” The words were a huge relief to finally say aloud, but it wouldn’t change things between him and Shane.

He’d told him to go fuck other people, live life, gain some experience, make memories, make mistakes even. All without Ilya. And not having Shane Hollander in his arms this weekend while he’s so close was going to be the hardest part.

“Oh, Ilyusha, come here.” Sveta quickly crossed over to him and pulled her friend into her arms as tears streamed down his face. It was no surprise to her that Ilya was in love with a man. But for it not to be reciprocated was the puzzling part.

How could you not love Ilya? Sure, he could be a bit of an asshole, but that was the front he put up to protect his softness.

Ilya had the biggest heart she knew. He loves so deeply, even when it’s not returned. Sometimes, especially when it’s not returned.

The sacrifices he made to keep his father, brother, sister-in-law, and niece comfortable went beyond duty. He gave them the best of everything, and it was never enough. And now some playboy had taken her best friend's heart and unceremoniously crushed it? Oh hell no. Not on Svetlana Vetrova’s watch.

“Who was it? Was it Sasha, because Sasha loves only Sasha.”

She stroked Ilya’s hair gently, working her fingers close to his scalp like his mother used to when they were children.

“No, it was someone else. And don’t go after him, please; he didn’t know. He’s a nice guy, rather clueless but in a charming way.”

Shane’s big, wet, brown eyes came to mind, and Ilya’s heart hurt even more. How was he going to stand playing with Shane? He can hardly think of him without bursting at the seams with love.

“Okay, but I’d like to have a few choice words with him. How could he not love you, Ilya! Anyone who’s spent more than an hour with you loves you.”

“Ehhh,” Ilya muses, preparing to counter Sveta, but she cuts in.

“We’re not talking about Grigori and Alexei, Ilya. Your friend Marleau loves you. Your mother loved you. I love you. Okay? This guy, who does not love you, he may need more time. He may already love you and is not ready to say so. But I cannot believe he doesn’t love you at all. It is not possible. You’re too lovable for that.”

Ilya’s sobs turned into a soft, quiet release as his tears slowed and his heart rate returned to a normal rhythm. Svetlana rubbed his back, one hand on the nape of Ilya’s neck, the other between his shoulders. He took a deep breath, pulled back, and kissed her lips in a gentle peck.

“I don’t deserve you, Sveta.”

“Yes, you do. And you deserve better than the mystery man who broke your heart.”

Ilya had to disagree with her, but he didn’t say so aloud. Sveta didn’t know Shane, and if she did, she’d know that she was wrong. There was nobody better than Shane.

All-Star Weekend

Ilya met Shane in the bar of the resort they were staying in. He looked amazing: the light colored suit he wore was simple but felt very Shane. The confidence with which he walked into the room was new for Ilya to witness. Coming out had been good for Shane, as Ilya had predicted.

Shane crossed the length of the room to join Ilya at the bar. Ilya exhaled slowly, willing his nerves to settle as he sipped his beer.

“You look pretty. Is this a new outfit?” Ilya asked as he not-so-subtly checked Shane out.

“Yeah, it is. I wanted a new look, and I figured this was a good time to show it off.”

Shane let his eyes rove over Ilya as he hunched over the counter. His tropical shirt was too bright to miss, and even under the loose fabric, Shane could see how tense Ilya was.

“It works for you. I’m sure you turn a lot of heads.” Ilya downed the rest of his beer and signaled the bartender for another.

“Make that two beers, please,” Shane interjected to Ilya’s surprise.

“Oh, so no ginger ale?”

“No, I’m feeling a bit wild today,” Shane said with a wink that made Ilya bite his lip.

“Who are you and what have you done with boring Shane Hollander?”

“Fuck off, Rozanov.” He chuckled before taking a swig of the beer the bartender placed in front of him.

Ilya hid his joy behind his signature smirk, glad that things weren’t going as bad as he feared.

“So…did you bring anyone with you?” Ilya probed cautiously.

“No, but I joined this app Grindr, but it’s missing an e. Anyway, they say Florida is very gay so, who knows? I might get lucky.”

Ilya was proud to see Shane spread his wings in the light for the world to see his gorgeous, freckled plumage. Why shouldn’t the world know the Shane Hollander he does? As long as Shane was happy, what else mattered?

“Then I wish you good luck, Captain. Should be an interesting weekend.” Ilya raised his beer to Shane, who clinked the neck of his bottle with Ilya’s.

“Oh, I have no doubts.”

Team East won handily over Team West, thanks to Shane and Ilya’s intuitive play style and support from Carter Vaughan. It was rocky at first, but after a whispered pep talk from Shane to Ilya during the first intermission, Ilya skated back on the ice a new man.

Once Ilya’s head was clear, he and Shane fell into an easy rhythm, passing the puck with lightning speed and precision. Two Months Later

Alexei had been calling nonstop. It seemed as soon as Ilya rejected one call, Alexei dialed again. He couldn’t deal with this bullshit right now. Ilya had a game tonight. Against Montreal. Against Shane. He needed to channel all of his attention into playing hockey and not pining for the man across from him in the faceoff. Ilya decided Alexei could wait until after the game, and then he’d see what the bastard wanted.

The game was as difficult as Ilya expected. Of course, Hollander was at the top of his game, riding a high he’d been on since coming out. The game was tied from the end of the second period and went into overtime when Ilya scored the winning goal, slipping the puck past Miitka with his signature move.

He went into the locker room on Cloud Nine and not even his demanding brother could bring him down. As expected, Alexei called as soon as Ilya retrieved his phone from his locker. When he did pick up, Ilya wished he hadn’t ignored Alexei’s first call. Their father had died that morning. With little notice to anyone but his coach, Ilya boarded the first flight to Moscow he could find.

It was 4 pm in Moscow when Ilya landed. Sveta picked him up from the airport and drove him to his apartment. The doctors had told Ilya his father didn’t have many years left when he received the diagnosis, that this was an aggressive form of dementia, so Ilya did what he could to make arrangements for his father’s burial.

He saved the documents in a file cabinet in his apartment, reviewing all the people he needed to call and errands he had to run to ensure the funeral met his father’s exacting standards. Even in death, Ilya dared not disappoint him.

After Svetlana had gone home for the night, Ilya’s phone rang with a familiar tone, “Secret” by Maroon 5. He lets the song play once, then picks up on the second ring.

“Ilya?”

Shane’s voice came through a little shaky, and it took Ilya back to when they started hooking up, the way Shane had been so nervous to name his desires. Ilya couldn’t help smiling, hearing that voice right now.

“Hi, Shane.”

“Where are you?” Shane asked, thinking about the gossip in the locker room after practice that Ilya hadn’t gone with his team to play in Nashville.

“I’m home. In Moscow.”

“Is your father…”

“Yes. He’s dead.” It was the first time Ilya had said it aloud. It felt so final, definite now that the words were out there.

“Ilya, I’m so sorry. What…what do you need?”

“Just talking is enough. What are they saying about me?”

Ilya knew his sudden departure had to raise suspicion. There was little the sports world loved more than a rumor.

“Nothing, just that you’re not with the team in Nashville.”

“Good. I’ll be back in a week.”

“Ilya, you should take more time than that.”

Ilya loved how concerned Shane was. He could count the people who checked in on him on one hand and Shane was the first on that list.

Usually, people would take more time for bereavement leave, but Ilya had to get back on the ice. He couldn’t sit in this space for long before it would swallow him whole, and he wasn’t going to let his grief win.

“It’s okay. I’ve already let my coach know when I’ll be back.”

“When’s the funeral?”

“In three days. But I can’t stay here, Shane. It’s too much. It’s too dark.”

“Okay, but I think the Raiders would give you more time if you asked for it. You’re their star Captain, they’d have to —”

“I know, Shane, but it doesn’t matter if I’m in Boston or Russia. It’s the grief. I can’t sit around doing nothing for long. It’s not good for me. I have to be busy. So I’ll be back on the ice in a week.”

“I get that. But you don’t have to carry it alone. You can talk to me anytime. And it doesn’t have to be about your father, it could just be the barista made your coffee wrong.” Ilya snorted, his usual smirk back on his face.

“That doesn’t happen as often as you’d think.”

“Well, my point stands. If it feels like it’s pulling you under, then call me. And we can just shoot the breeze.”

“Hollander, you’re making shit up now. What is ‘shoot the breeze’?” Now Shane was laughing on the other end of the call.

“It means just kill time, hang out. It’s an old phrase.”

“Old boring phrase. It fits you.”

“Fuck you, I’m not old! I’m barely older than you.”

“How would you know? I never told you my birthday.”

Shane blushed and mumbled into the phone.

“It was on Wikipedia.”

“Hmm? Speak up, I’m nine hours away, and I can’t hear you.”

“I said it was on Wikipedia,” Shane said a little more assuredly.

So Shane had looked him up, too. It made Ilya feel vindicated knowing Shane may’ve been as taken with him as he was with Shane.

“Is that all you learned from my Wikipedia page? Just my birthday?”

“Fuck you, Rozanov,” Shane retorted through a breathy laugh. “It must be pretty late for you, and you’ve had a long day.”

“Not that late. It’s almost ten o’clock.”

“Still, you need rest. Can I call you after the funeral then?”

“I was counting on it,” Ilya admitted. Sometimes the truth is so big it can’t help but slip out when he was vulnerable.

“Then I’ll call around the same time? Would that work?”

“Okay. Thank you, Shane.”

“You’re welcome, Ilya. Good night.”

“Spokoynoy nochi, Shane.”

Three Days Later

Spending three days with his family was the last thing Ilya wanted, but he was determined to see his father laid to rest exactly the way he desired. Alexei had the decency not to start any shit around guests, but he kept looking at Ilya with a mixture of disgust and contempt.

It all came to a head during the repast. Alexei berated Ilya, then insulted Sveta just for saying this argument could be heard in the next room. By the time Shane called, Ilya could use a listening ear.

“Hey, Ilya? Is this a good time?” Shane asked, considerate as always.

“It’s a perfect time. I’m back in my apartment. I don’t have to see my brother again. I’ll leave tomorrow and be a free man.”

“Would you like to talk more about that?”

“I don’t know. It’s a lot. I don’t want to burden you with it. You shouldn’t have to carry my problems.”

“Ilya, I want to. You seem to be carrying a lot, and I want to help.”

Shane’s earnestness pulled at Ilya’s heart again. All he wanted was to tell this man he loved him, but he wouldn’t. Not until Shane was sure he wanted Ilya. Not until Shane chose him. Instead, he tucked the confession away for safekeeping until the time was right. After a deep breath, Ilya let the day off his chest.

“My brother is…like my father but on coke. He hates me, but he’s always asking for money, and it’s never enough for him. He complains that I wasn’t there for our father, but I paid for everything. Food, clothes, the whole fucking funeral, I paid for.

“And I wanted to help, I wanted to be the one taking care of my father. But I was dying here, Shane. I had to leave. And playing for Boston helped my family, so…you’d think he’d be satisfied, but no. So, I’m giving Alexei this apartment, and I’m setting up a trust for my niece. And tomorrow I wash my hands of him.”

Shane was silent for a while, and Ilya wasn’t sure if they were still on the call.

“Shane? Are you still there?”

“Oh, yeah. I’m sorry. That sounds horrible.” His voice broke on the word “horrible,” a sniffle coming through the speaker.

“Shane, are you crying?”

Ilya knew the answer was yes, and more than ever he yearned to pull Shane into his arms, wipe his tears, and comfort him.

“No,” came Shane’s wet reply.

“Shane, I’m okay. I’ll be okay, I promise. You don’t need to worry about me.”

“Okay. If you’re sure, Ilya.”

“I am sure. Now tell me about Grindr. Anything: guys you’ve met, bad dates, good dates, hookups, you name it, I want to hear it.”

Shane laughed and launched into a series of misadventures of Grindr hookups, ending with a story about the guy whose ex-wife crashed their dinner date. Soon, they both were laughing, the pain from the previous conversation momentarily forgotten.

“The crazy part is, she was pretty cool! He was leaning over, asking me ‘How about we ditch the hag and fuck in my truck?’ I had to leave after that. Turned me off like that,” Shane emphasized with a snap of his fingers.

“I don’t blame you. He sounds like a dick.”

“Not a dick I want, no thanks!”

“Yes, you deserve much better dick than that.” Ilya smiled, recalling how much this sounded like a conversation he had with Sveta earlier in the year.

“Thanks, I’m trying to get some, but I think I’ll leave Grindr alone for a while. Maybe I’ll meet someone the old-fashioned way: in a bar.”

Shane and Ilya shared one more laugh as the call reached a natural endpoint.

“I should let you go to sleep. You’ve got a flight to catch.”

“Yes, I do. At least there’s not much to pack. Thank you, Shane. For calling. It really helped.”

“What are friends for? Good night, Ilya. Have a safe flight.”

“Thank you. Good night, Shane.”
